Industrial Machine Component Selection & Verification

Selecting the right standard mechanical components is the difference between a high-performance industrial system and premature machine failure. At Susilya, we follow a rigorous mathematical and application-driven engineering approach to evaluate, select, and integrate critical machine elements.

1. Industrial Bearing Selection & Life Calculations

Bearings are the core of rotary systems. Choosing the right bearing requires mapping out the exact mechanical application constraints to prevent operational fatigue.

Critical Engineering Parameters Covered:

  • Load Types: Evaluating pure radial, pure axial, or combined moment loads to choose between Deep Groove, Angular Contact, Tapered Roller, or Thrust bearings.
  • L10 Life Calculations: Calculating the rating life in hours or revolutions using dynamic load ratings ($C$) and equivalent applied loads ($P$).
  • Lubrication & Sealing: Selecting oil vs. grease lubrication based on operational DN factors (speed metrics) and working environment contaminations.

2. Precision Ball Screw Selection & Sizing

For high-accuracy linear actuation, ball screws convert rotary motion to linear displacement with minimal friction. Correct sizing prevents buckling and positioning errors.

Critical Engineering Parameters Covered:

  • Lead Accuracy & Backlash: Matching international tolerance classes (e.g., C3, C5, C7) with the precision requirements of the machine.
  • Critical Speed Verification: Calculating the maximum allowable rotational speed to avoid dangerous screw shaft whipping (resonance).
  • Buckling Load Limits: Verifying the structural safety of the screw under high compressive axial loads, depending on end-mounting arrangements (Fixed-Supported, Fixed-Fixed, etc.).

3. Linear Guide Profile Rails & Carriage Integration

Profile linear guide rails support linear motion vectors while carrying heavy moments and loads. Sizing ensures system structural stiffness.

Critical Engineering Parameters Covered:

  • Preload Classes: Selecting light, medium, or heavy preload settings to maximize machine rigidity without causing excessive operational drag.
  • Static Safety Factors: Testing linear blocks against sudden impact loads, emergency stops, and heavy overhung moment loads.
  • Parallelism & Mounting Geometric Tolerances: Engineering precise mounting shoulder heights and tolerances to avoid internal binding of carriages.
